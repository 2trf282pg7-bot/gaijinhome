Why Is Your Apartment Lease Renewal Being Denied in Japan?

Updated November 2026 · GaijinHome

You have lived in your apartment for almost two years, paid rent on time, and assumed renewal would be a formality. Then a letter arrives from the management company saying the contract will not be renewed, or the guarantor company says it will not continue your coverage, or the renewal paperwork asks for documents you cannot produce in time. Suddenly you are facing a possible move with a few months' notice, in a language you do not fully read. Take a breath. In many cases, a tenant in Japan has more protection than they realize, and the first job is to figure out exactly what kind of "no" you received.

What Actually Happens Around Renewal Time

Most residential leases in Japan run for two years. A few months before the end date, the management company (管理会社) usually sends a renewal notice (更新のお知らせ) with a new contract or a renewal agreement (更新契約書), a request for the renewal fee, and sometimes a request to update your details with the guarantor company. Problems for foreign tenants tend to show up in one of four ways:

  • The landlord says they will not renew. Often phrased as "the owner plans to use the property" or "we are not renewing contracts in this building."
  • Your lease turns out to be a fixed-term lease (定期借家契約). These end on the stated date by design, and renewal is not automatic.
  • The guarantor company declines to continue. This can happen if your visa has become shorter, your job changed, or there were late payments.
  • The renewal terms change. A higher rent, a new renewal fee, or a new requirement such as switching guarantor companies, which then rejects you.

Each of these has a different fix, so read your current contract first. The contract type is usually written at the top: 普通建物賃貸借契約 means an ordinary lease, and 定期建物賃貸借契約 means a fixed-term lease.

Why Renewal Gets Denied: The Structural Reasons

Ordinary leases are strongly protected

Under Japan's Act on Land and Building Leases (借地借家法), a landlord with an ordinary lease cannot simply refuse to renew. They need a justifiable reason (正当事由), and they must give notice within a set window before the lease ends (generally between one year and six months beforehand). Courts look at factors such as the landlord's need to use the property and whether compensation (立退料) is offered. If the landlord does nothing, the lease generally continues by statutory renewal (法定更新), even without a new signed contract. This is the single most important point for anyone receiving a "we will not renew" letter.

Fixed-term leases end on schedule

Fixed-term leases are increasingly common for foreign tenants, corporate housing, and properties the owner plans to sell or rebuild. They can end without a justifiable reason, provided the landlord followed the required procedure (including a separate written explanation at signing and, for terms of a year or more, an end-of-term notice). You can still ask for a new contract (再契約), but the landlord is free to say no.

The guarantor company reassesses you

Many guarantor company contracts renew every one or two years, often with a renewal fee. When your situation changes, such as a shorter residence period, a new employer, or a period of unemployment, the guarantor company may decline to continue. Some management companies then treat this as a reason not to renew, or ask you to find a new guarantor.

Your residence status looks shorter than the new term

If your residence card expires before the new contract would end, the landlord may hesitate. This is especially common for people whose visa renewal is still pending at immigration, or who have recently changed status.

Complaints or rule breaches

Noise complaints, garbage sorting issues, unauthorized roommates, pets, or subletting can all be cited. Depending on how serious and documented they are, these may or may not amount to a justifiable reason, but they make negotiation harder.

Common Mistakes Tenants Make

  • Accepting the letter as final. With an ordinary lease, a non-renewal notice is the start of a negotiation, not a court order.
  • Signing a new fixed-term contract without reading it. Some landlords offer to "renew" by switching you to a fixed-term lease, which gives you less protection next time.
  • Ignoring the guarantor company paperwork. Missing the guarantor renewal payment or forms can create a breach that gives the landlord a stronger case.
  • Moving out quickly without discussing compensation. If the landlord needs you out of an ordinary lease, moving costs or compensation are often part of the discussion.
  • Stopping rent payments in protest. This turns you into the party in breach. Keep paying.

Step-by-Step Fix

  1. Identify your contract type. Find your original lease and look for 普通 (ordinary) or 定期 (fixed-term) in the title. If you cannot find it, ask the management company for a copy: 「賃貸借契約書の写しをいただけますか。」
  2. Ask for the reason in writing. Politely request a written explanation of why the lease will not be renewed. Say: "Could you tell me the reason for non-renewal in writing?" (更新しない理由を書面で教えていただけますか。) This helps you, and helps any advisor you consult later.
  3. If it is an ordinary lease, state that you wish to continue. Reply in writing that you want to keep living there and continue paying rent. Keep paying on time and keep receipts or bank records.
  4. If the guarantor company is the problem, offer solutions. Ask whether another guarantor company can be used, whether a larger deposit would help, or whether an updated certificate of employment and residence card would allow them to continue.
  5. If your visa renewal is pending, show proof. Bring the receipt stamp or notice from immigration showing your application is in progress, plus a letter from your employer confirming continued employment.
  6. Get free advice before agreeing to leave. Many prefectures and cities offer housing or legal consultations, and the Japan Legal Support Center (法テラス) provides information and, in some cases, multilingual support. A consultation can tell you whether the landlord's reason is likely to hold up.
  7. If you must move, negotiate the exit. Ask for enough time, help with moving costs, and a clear written agreement about the deposit refund. Read our guide on disputing non-refundable deposit deductions before you sign anything.
  8. Line up a fallback home early. If time is short, a furnished option can buy you breathing room while you search properly. [PR] CrossOneRoom furnished apartments in Tokyo are set up for foreign tenants, and our monthly mansion guide covers short-term options elsewhere.
Key tip: If your lease is an ordinary lease and the landlord has not shown a justifiable reason, keep paying rent and put your wish to stay in writing. Silence and an early move-out are the two things that weaken your position most.

Required Documents for Renewal or Negotiation

  • Original lease contract (賃貸借契約書) and any important matters explanation (重要事項説明書)
  • Renewal notice or non-renewal letter from the management company (更新のお知らせ / 更新拒絶通知)
  • Guarantor company contract and renewal notice (保証委託契約書)
  • Current residence card (在留カード), front and back
  • Proof of pending visa renewal, if applicable (immigration application receipt)
  • Updated certificate of employment (在職証明書)
  • Tax certificate (課税証明書) or withholding tax slip (源泉徴収票) for income verification
  • Rent payment records (bank statements or transfer receipts)
  • Any written communication about complaints or rule issues

City and Region Differences

Tokyo and the surrounding Kanto region commonly charge a renewal fee (更新料), typically around one month's rent every two years, plus a guarantor company renewal fee and sometimes an administrative fee to the agent. Redevelopment is also active in many central wards, which can lead to non-renewal of older buildings slated for rebuilding. In these cases, compensation discussions are common.

Osaka and much of Kansai have historically been less likely to charge a renewal fee, though practices vary and newer contracts may include one. Kyoto is known for renewal fees in many contracts. See our Osaka guide for local contract conventions.

Regional cities often have more owner-managed properties. Renewal decisions may come directly from the owner, which can make a face-to-face conversation more effective than letters. Fixed-term leases are also used for company-leased housing in some regional areas, so check whether your employer, not you, is the actual tenant.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my landlord refuse to renew just because I am a foreigner?

For an ordinary lease, nationality alone is unlikely to be accepted as a justifiable reason for non-renewal, since the landlord needs a legitimate reason such as their own need to use the property. Fixed-term leases are different because they end on schedule. If you suspect your nationality is the real reason, get a consultation before agreeing to move.

What happens if I do not sign the renewal contract?

For an ordinary lease, the tenancy generally continues under statutory renewal (法定更新) on similar terms, even without a newly signed contract. Whether you still owe a renewal fee in that situation depends on your contract wording and is a common source of disputes, so check the clause and ask for advice if unsure.

My guarantor company will not renew. Do I have to move out?

Not automatically. Ask the management company whether another guarantor company is acceptable, or whether a higher deposit or updated documents would allow you to continue. Our guide on what to do when a guarantor refuses you covers alternatives.

Can the landlord raise my rent at renewal?

The landlord can propose an increase, but for an ordinary lease you do not have to accept it automatically. If you disagree, you can generally continue paying the current rent while discussing it. Large disputes over rent amounts can go to mediation. Keep everything in writing.

How much notice must the landlord give?

For an ordinary lease, the landlord generally must notify you of non-renewal between one year and six months before the lease ends. For fixed-term leases of a year or more, a notice of the end of the term is also required in a similar window. If you received notice later than that, mention it when you consult an advisor.
