Apartment Rejected in Fukuoka as a Foreigner? English-Speaking Solutions That Work

Updated November 2026 · GaijinHome

Fukuoka has a reputation as one of Japan's most livable cities: compact, affordable, close to the airport, and increasingly international thanks to its startup scene and universities. So it can feel especially discouraging when an agent in Tenjin politely tells you the landlord "does not accept foreigners," or when your application comes back declined after a week of waiting. The city is easier than Tokyo in many ways, but it has its own bottlenecks, and most of them come down to language support and how local landlords manage risk. Here is how to get past them.

What Actually Happens When You Search in Fukuoka

The typical story goes like this. You find listings on a major portal, contact an agent near Tenjin or Hakata Station, and arrive for a viewing. The agent speaks limited English, so communication is slow. Some properties you saved are suddenly "already taken," which is sometimes true and sometimes a soft way of saying the owner prefers Japanese tenants. You apply for one that is available, and a few days later the guarantor company or the owner declines.

Compared with Tokyo, Fukuoka has fewer agencies with dedicated English staff, and a larger share of buildings are owned by individual landlords who manage their own risk. That means decisions often depend on how comfortable the owner feels with communication, not just on your income.

Why It Happens: Fukuoka-Specific Reasons

Language and emergency-contact concerns

The most common worry for local landlords is not payment but communication: who will they call if there is a water leak, a noise complaint, or a problem with garbage sorting? If your application lacks a Japanese-speaking emergency contact (緊急連絡先) in Japan, it is a red flag for many owners in Fukuoka.

Guarantor companies with Japanese-only processes

Many guarantor companies (保証会社) used in Kyushu verify applicants by phone in Japanese. If you cannot answer the verification call, or your employer's contact is unreachable, the screening can fail on a technicality.

Students and graduating residents

Fukuoka has a large international student population at institutions such as Kyushu University, Fukuoka University and Seinan Gakuin University, plus many language schools. Landlords near campuses often see students leave at short notice after graduation, so they look closely at residence period and financial backing. If your student visa expires within a year, expect extra questions.

Startup founders and freelancers

Fukuoka is known for its startup support programs, which attract founders and remote workers. Unfortunately, newly founded companies and self-employed income are hard for guarantor companies to verify, so founders sometimes fail screening even with savings in the bank.

Strong demand in central wards

Chuo-ku and Hakata-ku are popular with both locals and newcomers. When several applicants want the same unit, owners tend to pick the one that looks lowest risk on paper.

Common Mistakes

  • Searching only in Tenjin and Hakata. These are the most competitive areas. Equally convenient neighborhoods one or two subway stops away can be easier and cheaper.
  • Walking into random agencies without checking English support. Call or email ahead to ask whether they handle foreign tenants regularly.
  • Skipping the emergency contact field. In Fukuoka this matters more than in many big-city applications.
  • Not preparing a short Japanese self-introduction. A few lines about your job, how long you plan to stay, and that you understand building rules can reassure a hesitant owner.

Step-by-Step Fix

  1. Ask the agent to pre-check foreigner acceptance. Before viewing, ask: 「この物件は外国籍の方でも申し込みできますか？」 ("Can foreign nationals apply for this property?"). This saves you from viewing places that were never possible.
  2. Use support from the city or your institution. Universities in Fukuoka typically have international student offices that can advise on housing or act as a contact. The city's international association, Fukuoka International Association (Rainbow Plaza), offers information for foreign residents. Employers' HR teams often help with housing as well.
  3. Secure a Japanese-speaking emergency contact. A colleague, supervisor, school staff member, or Japanese friend can usually serve. Let them know they may get a short confirmation call.
  4. Prepare a one-page profile in Japanese and English. Include your employer, position, income, length of stay, and a line saying you will follow garbage and building rules. Hand it to the agent to send with the application.
  5. If you are a founder or freelancer, front-load proof. Bring tax filing records, business registration documents, and a bank balance certificate. If your company can sign the lease as a corporate contract (法人契約), screening may be easier.
  6. Widen your search area. Consider neighborhoods along the subway lines beyond the center (see below). Rents drop and owners are often more flexible.
  7. Use a no-guarantor bridge if needed. UR public housing has developments in the Fukuoka area and requires no guarantor or key money; see our UR housing guide. Share houses and monthly furnished apartments are also good short-term options while you build a local track record; our monthly mansion guide explains how these work.
  8. Reapply with a different guarantor company. If the rejection came from the guarantor, ask the agent whether a property managed by a different company is available. Each company screens differently. More detail in our no-guarantor guide.
Key tip: In Fukuoka, owners decide based on trust as much as numbers. A clear Japanese-speaking emergency contact and a short self-introduction often do more than an extra pay slip.

Required Documents

  • Residence card (在留カード), front and back
  • Passport (パスポート)
  • Certificate of employment (在職証明書) or job offer letter (内定通知書)
  • Recent pay slips (給与明細) or tax certificate (課税証明書)
  • Certificate of enrollment (在学証明書) and student ID (学生証) for students
  • Proof of scholarship or sponsor funds for students (奨学金証明書 / 送金証明)
  • Business registration (登記事項証明書) and tax return copy (確定申告書の控え) for founders and freelancers
  • Bank balance certificate (残高証明書)
  • Emergency contact details in Japan (緊急連絡先)
  • Japanese mobile number

Fukuoka Neighborhoods, Rents and Local Quirks

Fukuoka's rents are generally well below Tokyo's. As a rough guide, a studio or 1K in a central area often falls somewhere in the 40,000–70,000 yen range depending on age, size and distance from a station, while family-sized units cost more. Always check current listings, as prices move.

Local quirks: Many buildings in Fukuoka are owner-managed, so face-to-face impressions count. Some older contracts in Kyushu use deposit conventions where part of the deposit is deducted by agreement, so read the deposit section carefully; our hidden costs guide explains common fees. Parking is often separate and can matter if you plan to drive. Summers are humid, so ask about mold history and ventilation in older ground-floor units.

Timing: Fukuoka's rental market gets busiest from roughly January to March, when students and new employees move ahead of the April start of the academic and business year. During this period good units disappear quickly and owners can afford to be picky. If you can, search in late spring, summer or autumn, when there is less competition and agents have more time to help you. If you must move in peak season, have every document ready before your first viewing so you can apply the same day.

Compared with Tokyo and Osaka: Fukuoka has less competition and lower rents, but far fewer English-speaking agencies. In Tokyo you can often find a fully English service; in Fukuoka you may need to rely more on personal support networks. For comparison, see our Osaka guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there English-speaking real estate agents in Fukuoka?

Some agencies in Tenjin and Hakata have staff who speak English or work regularly with foreign tenants, but they are fewer than in Tokyo. Ask your employer, school or other foreign residents for recommendations, and contact agencies in advance to confirm language support before visiting.

Is it easier to rent in Fukuoka than in Tokyo?

Rents are lower and competition is lighter, which helps with income-based screening. However, owner-managed buildings and limited English support can make the process feel harder. Strong preparation, especially a Japanese-speaking emergency contact, usually makes the difference.

Can I rent an apartment in Fukuoka before I arrive in Japan?

It is difficult for standard leases, because most require a residence card and a Japanese phone number. Many newcomers stay in a monthly furnished apartment or share house for the first weeks, then apply for a regular lease once they have their residence card and bank account.

I am on a startup visa. Will landlords accept me?

Some will, but newly founded businesses are hard for guarantor companies to verify. Bring business registration documents, a business plan summary, and a bank balance certificate, and consider asking whether a corporate contract is possible. A short-term furnished option can bridge the gap while your company builds a record.

What should I do right after a rejection in Fukuoka?

Ask the agent whether the owner or the guarantor company declined, fix the weakest point (often the emergency contact or income proof), and try a property managed by a different company or in a less competitive neighborhood. Our rejection recovery page has more steps.
