UR Housing Application Denied for Foreigners in Japan? Exact Reasons and Fixes

Updated November 2026 · GaijinHome

UR housing (UR賃貸住宅) is often described as the easiest way for foreigners to rent in Japan: no guarantor, no key money, no agent fee, and no renewal fee. So it is a real shock when the UR sales office tells you your application cannot be accepted. The reassuring part is that UR's screening is rule-based rather than based on a landlord's personal preference. If you were turned down, there is almost always a specific, checkable reason, and many of them can be fixed within days.

What Actually Happens When UR Says No

UR is operated by the Urban Renaissance Agency, a public corporation. Instead of a private guarantor company deciding whether you are a risk, UR applies published eligibility standards. You visit a UR sales center (UR営業センター) or a property's on-site office, choose a unit, and submit documents. Staff check them against the criteria, sometimes on the spot.

Rejections typically happen in one of these ways:

  • Staff tell you at the counter that your income does not meet the standard for that unit.
  • Your documents are missing or not the right type, so the application cannot proceed.
  • Your residence status or registration does not meet the requirements.
  • The unit you wanted was reserved by someone else while you gathered documents.

Unlike private rentals, UR staff will usually explain which requirement was not met. That makes this one of the more fixable rejections you can face in Japan.

Why It Happens: How UR Screening Works

The income standard is tied to rent

UR's main test is whether your average monthly income meets a multiple of the unit's rent. For most units, the standard is roughly four times the monthly rent, with fixed income thresholds applied to higher-rent units. If you chose a unit just above what your income supports, you will be declined for that unit but may qualify for a cheaper one in the same complex. Always check the current standard with UR, as details can change.

How income is measured

UR generally looks at documented annual income, such as a withholding tax slip (源泉徴収票) or tax certificate (課税証明書). If you arrived in Japan recently and have no Japanese tax record yet, you may need to use another proof such as a UR-format income certificate from your employer, which newcomers often do not know about.

Savings or prepayment instead of income

If your income does not meet the standard, UR has alternatives. The savings standard (貯蓄基準制度) accepts applicants with savings of around 100 times the monthly rent. A lump-sum prepayment option (一時払い制度) lets you pay a year of rent and common service fees in advance without income proof. Many applicants are rejected simply because nobody mentioned these options.

Residence status and registration

Foreign applicants generally need to be mid- to long-term residents with a residence card (在留カード) and be registered as a resident in Japan (住民登録). Short-term visitors cannot apply. If you have just arrived and not yet registered your address at the city office, your application may stall.

Household composition

UR asks who will live in the unit. Some units or programs have requirements about household members, and applicants must generally be living as a household in Japan. Listing a partner or relative who is not yet in Japan, or whose status is unclear, can complicate things.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing the unit before checking the income threshold. Ask staff for the maximum rent you qualify for first, then pick a unit.
  • Bringing pay slips instead of a tax document. UR usually wants annual income proof, not just monthly slips.
  • Not knowing the savings or prepayment options. These exist specifically for people whose income on paper is not enough.
  • Applying before registering your address. Complete resident registration at your city or ward office first.
  • Using a "Shachihata" ink stamp. UR may require a proper personal seal (認印), not a self-inking stamp.

Step-by-Step Fix

  1. Ask exactly which requirement failed. At the counter, ask: 「どの基準を満たしていないか教えていただけますか？」 ("Could you tell me which standard I did not meet?"). Write down the answer.
  2. If income was the issue, ask for your maximum rent. Say: 「私の収入で申し込める家賃の上限はいくらですか？」 ("What is the maximum rent I can apply for with my income?"). Then look for units in that range, including in nearby UR complexes.
  3. Use the savings standard if you have savings. Prepare a bank balance certificate (残高証明書) showing savings of about 100 times the monthly rent, and confirm with UR which accounts are accepted.
  4. Consider the prepayment option. If you have cash but no Japanese income record, ask about 一時払い制度, which lets you prepay a year's rent and common service charges.
  5. If you are new in Japan, ask about the UR-format income certificate. Your employer may be able to fill in UR's own form to confirm your expected salary. Ask the sales center for the correct form.
  6. Fix residence and registration issues. Register your address at the city office, bring your residence card, and if your visa expires soon, apply for renewal and bring proof it is in progress.
  7. Reserve, then gather documents quickly. UR may let you hold a unit for a short period while you collect paperwork. Ask how long, and prioritize the documents that take longest, like tax certificates.

Key tip: Before you fall in love with a UR unit, ask the staff what maximum rent your income supports. Choosing within that limit avoids the most common UR rejection entirely.

Required Documents

  • Residence card (在留カード), front and back
  • Certificate of residence (住民票) showing your registered address and household members
  • Income proof: withholding tax slip (源泉徴収票) or tax certificate (課税証明書)
  • UR-format income certificate (収入証明書) from your employer, if you are new to Japan
  • Proof of employment, such as certificate of employment (在職証明書) or employee ID
  • Bank balance certificate (残高証明書), if using the savings standard
  • Photo ID showing your address, such as My Number card (マイナンバーカード) or driver's license
  • Personal seal (認印), not a self-inking stamp
  • Student or sponsor documents, if applicable (confirm eligibility with UR)

City and Region Differences

Tokyo: UR has many complexes in the Tokyo area, from older large estates in the suburbs to newer towers in more central locations. Central units can have high rents, which raises the income threshold. Suburban estates along major train lines often offer larger units at lower rents. Popular units can go quickly, so check availability often. Some UR offices in central Tokyo have staff who can help in English, but bring Japanese support if you can.

Osaka and Kansai: The Osaka area has a large UR presence, including big estates in places like the northern suburbs and around the bay area. Rents are generally lower than Tokyo's, which makes the income standard easier to meet. For local context, see our Osaka guide.

Nagoya, Fukuoka and other regions: UR complexes exist in many major metropolitan areas, though supply is smaller. Units can be more spacious for the price. Fewer offices have English support, so prepare documents carefully before you visit.

A practical note on location: many of the most affordable UR units are in large estates (団地) built decades ago. They are often well maintained and spacious, but may have no elevator in low-rise buildings and be a bus ride from the station. Newer or renovated units cost more and therefore need higher income. Viewing both types helps you balance rent, commute and the income standard.

Across all regions, UR also runs campaigns that can reduce costs for certain groups, such as young people or families, or for certain units. Ask about current campaigns, but do not count on them, as they change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners really apply for UR housing without a guarantor?

Yes. UR does not require a guarantor or a guarantor company for its standard rentals. Foreign residents who meet the residence and income or savings requirements can apply on the same basis as Japanese applicants. This is why UR is often recommended after a private rental rejection; see our rejection recovery guide.

Can I apply for UR before I arrive in Japan?

In general, UR expects applicants to be registered residents in Japan with a residence card. There are procedures for some people moving back from overseas, but most newcomers will need to arrive, register their address, and then apply. A short-term furnished stay is common in the meantime; see our monthly mansion guide.

I am a student. Can I rent from UR?

Students may be able to apply if they meet the income, savings or prepayment requirements, but many do not have enough income on paper. Ask the sales center about the savings standard or prepayment option, and whether any student-related programs are available in your area.

What costs do I pay when moving into UR?

Typically a deposit of around two months' rent, the first month's rent (often prorated), and the monthly common service fee. There is no key money, agent fee or renewal fee for standard UR rentals. Check the exact amounts for your unit, as they vary.

If UR rejects me once, can I apply again?

Yes. Because UR applies fixed standards, you can apply again as soon as you meet them, for example by choosing a cheaper unit, submitting savings proof, or using the prepayment option. There is no lasting penalty for being declined.
