Residence Card Renewal Held Up by Wrong Documents in Japan? What to Do Before Your Status Expires

You queued at the immigration bureau, handed over your papers, and then heard the words nobody wants to hear: "This document is not correct," or "Please submit additional materials." Maybe you received a letter a few weeks later asking for something you have never heard of. Maybe you were sent home from the counter without your application being accepted at all.

This is one of the most stressful situations a foreign resident can face in Japan, because your residence card (在留カード, zairyu card) is tied to almost everything else: your job, your bank account, your phone contract and, very often, your apartment. The good news is that a document problem is usually fixable, and in most cases it does not mean your renewal will be denied. What matters is understanding exactly where you are in the process and acting quickly.

What actually happens

When you apply to extend your period of stay (在留期間更新許可申請, zairyu kikan koshin kyoka shinsei), a document problem can show up at three different points, and each one means something different.

  • At the counter, before the application is accepted. Staff check your papers and refuse to take the application because something essential is missing or clearly wrong, for example a tax certificate for the wrong year, an unsigned form, or a photo that does not meet the size rules. In this case, you have not applied yet. Nothing is stamped on your card and the clock on your current status keeps running.
  • At the counter, application accepted but incomplete. Sometimes staff accept the application and tell you to send a missing item later. Your card receives a stamp on the back showing the application is in progress (申請中, shinsei-chu). You are now an applicant.
  • Weeks later, by post. Immigration reviews your file and mails you a request for additional materials (資料提出通知書, shiryo teishutsu tsuchisho). It lists what they want and a deadline. This is very common and is not a rejection.

The difference between the first case and the other two is critical. If your application was accepted before your current period of stay ended, you are generally allowed to remain in Japan while it is being processed, up to the date of the decision or two months after your original expiry date, whichever comes first. This is often called the "special period" (特例期間, tokurei kikan). If your application was never accepted, you get no such protection.

Check the back of your residence card right now. If there is no "application in progress" stamp and your expiry date is close, your top priority is getting a complete application accepted before that date. Everything else in this article comes second.

Why it happens

Most people whose renewal is held up did not do anything dishonest. The problems are structural, and knowing them helps you fix the real issue rather than just resubmitting the same file.

Requirements depend on your status, your employer and your history

There is no single document list. An engineer at a large listed company, a language school student, a spouse of a Japanese national and a business owner each need very different evidence. Immigration also sorts employers into categories, and a small or newly founded company usually has to submit far more paperwork than a large one. People often download a list meant for someone else.

Tax certificates are confusing

Many statuses require a certificate showing your residence tax amount (住民税の課税証明書, kazei shomeisho) and a certificate showing it has been paid (納税証明書, nozei shomeisho). These come from your city or ward office, not the tax office, and they are issued for a specific fiscal year based on the previous calendar year's income. Getting the wrong year, getting only one of the two, or having a certificate that shows unpaid tax is one of the most common reasons for a request for more documents.

Life changes that were never reported

If you changed jobs, left a school, moved, or divorced, there are notifications you were supposed to file, usually within 14 days. When your renewal file shows a different employer from the one immigration has on record, they will want an explanation and proof of the new job.

Social insurance and pension gaps

Immigration looks at whether you are meeting your basic obligations as a resident. Unpaid National Health Insurance (国民健康保険) or National Pension (国民年金) contributions can lead to questions and requests for proof of payment.

Language and timing

Forms, certificates and notices are in Japanese. It is easy to miss a signature box, to misread a deadline, or to throw away a letter from immigration because it looked like an advertisement. And because many people apply in the last weeks before expiry, there is little room to fix anything.

Common mistakes

  1. Waiting until the last week. In most cases you can apply from about three months before your period of stay ends. Applying early gives you time to fix a rejected document without the risk of falling out of status.
  2. Ignoring or missing the additional materials letter. If you do not respond by the deadline, immigration can decide based on what they already have, which often means a refusal. If you have moved, the letter may go to an old address.
  3. Submitting the wrong tax year or only one tax certificate. Ask the city office clearly for the most recent year and for both the taxation and payment certificates.
  4. Resubmitting a "fixed" document without explaining the problem. If a gap, a late tax payment or a job change caused the issue, a short written explanation (理由書, riyusho) attached to the documents usually helps more than silence.
  5. Telling your landlord nothing. Your guarantor company and management company may ask for a copy of your current card at renewal or when your card expires. Surprising them with an expired-looking card creates a second problem.

Step-by-step fix

  1. Identify which situation you are in. Was the application accepted (stamp on the back of your card, receipt or processing number) or sent back? If you are not sure, call the immigration bureau where you applied, or the Immigration Services Agency's information center, and ask: "Was my extension application accepted, and is my file complete?" In Japanese: "Kōshin shinsei wa uketsukeraremashita ka? Tarinai shorui wa arimasu ka?"
  2. Get the exact list of what is wrong, in writing if possible. If you received a letter, it lists the documents and the deadline. If you were turned away at the counter, ask staff to write down or point to the item on the official checklist. Take a photo of the checklist.
  3. Get the right certificates from the right offices.
    • Tax certificates and residence record (住民票, juminhyo): your city or ward office. Many can also be printed at convenience stores if you have a My Number Card.
    • Employment certificate (在職証明書, zaishoku shomeisho): your company's HR or general affairs department.
    • Enrollment and attendance certificates: your school office.
    Say what it is for: "Biza kōshin no tame desu" (it is for my visa renewal). Offices deal with this request every day.
  4. Fix the underlying issue, not just the paper. If a certificate shows unpaid residence tax or pension, pay it first, then get an updated payment certificate. If you never reported a job change, file the notification now and keep the receipt.
  5. Write a short explanation letter. One page, in simple Japanese or English, stating what was missing or wrong, why, and what you have done to fix it. Sign and date it. Honesty matters more than polish.
  6. Submit before the deadline, and keep proof. Follow the instructions in the letter. Many requests can be answered by post; use a trackable method and keep copies of everything you send. If you need more time to obtain a document, contact the bureau before the deadline and ask; do not just let it pass.
  7. If your application was never accepted, reapply immediately. Bring the complete set, arrive early, and if your expiry date is within days, say so at the counter. Check whether the online residence application system is available for your status and employer, as it can save a trip.
  8. Protect your housing and daily life. Tell your management company or guarantor company that your renewal is in process, and send them a copy of both sides of your card showing the "application in progress" stamp. Do the same if your bank or phone carrier asks. If paperwork in Japanese is the real bottleneck, a few sessions with a tutor focused on official forms can save you repeat trips: [PR] find a Japanese tutor on italki.
  9. Get professional help if the problem is serious. If you have overstayed, have a past refusal, or the issue involves a job that may not match your status, consult a licensed immigration lawyer (行政書士, gyoseishoshi, or a lawyer). Your local international association often offers free consultation days.

Required documents

The exact list depends on your status of residence and, for workers, your employer's category. Always confirm against the official checklist for your status. The items below cover the most common cases.

  • Application form for extension of period of stay (在留期間更新許可申請書), signed, with the employer or school section completed if required
  • One photo, 4 cm × 3 cm, taken within the last three months, meeting the official specifications
  • Passport (パスポート)
  • Residence card (在留カード)
  • Residence tax taxation certificate (住民税の課税証明書) for the most recent year
  • Residence tax payment certificate (住民税の納税証明書) for the same year
  • Employment certificate (在職証明書) or employment contract, for work statuses
  • Certificate of enrollment (在学証明書) and attendance record (出席証明書), for students
  • Residence record of your household (住民票), and marriage-related documents and a guarantee letter (身元保証書), for spouse statuses
  • Proof of health insurance and pension payments, if requested
  • A short explanation letter (理由書) if something in your file needs context
  • Copies of anything you have already submitted, and the additional materials notice if you received one

A revenue stamp for the processing fee is paid when the new card is issued after approval. Check the current amount on the Immigration Services Agency website, as fees have been revised in recent years.

City / region differences

The rules are national, but your experience depends heavily on which bureau handles your address.

  • Tokyo. The Tokyo Regional Immigration Services Bureau in Shinagawa is famously crowded, especially in the months before and after April. Arriving early in the morning makes a real difference, and long waits make the online application option worth checking. Residents of some areas may be covered by branch offices, so confirm your jurisdiction first.
  • Yokohama. Kanagawa residents are generally handled by the Yokohama branch rather than Shinagawa. Waits can still be long, but it is usually less overwhelming.
  • Osaka and Kobe. The Osaka Regional Immigration Services Bureau serves much of the Kansai area, with a branch in Kobe. If you live in Osaka, see our Osaka housing page for how local agencies treat applicants whose renewal is pending.
  • Nagoya, Fukuoka and Kyoto. Nagoya and Fukuoka have their own regional bureaus. Kyoto residents typically use a branch office. Smaller offices can be less crowded but may have shorter opening hours.

City offices also differ: some ward offices in large cities issue tax certificates the same day at a dedicated counter, while smaller towns may require you to request certificates by post if you have moved since January 1 of the relevant year. Remember that tax certificates come from the municipality where you lived on January 1, not necessarily where you live now.

Landlords and agencies differ too. Larger management companies in Tokyo and Osaka are used to tenants mid-renewal and usually accept a card with an "application in progress" stamp. Smaller, individual landlords may not understand it, so a short note in Japanese explaining the special period helps. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a request for additional documents mean my renewal will be denied?

No. Requests for more materials are a normal part of the review and are sent to many applicants. What matters is that you respond fully and by the deadline. Ignoring the request is what usually leads to a refusal.

My card expires next week and my application was sent back. What can I do?

Treat it as urgent. Gather the missing items the same day if possible and return to the bureau with a complete application before your expiry date. Tell the counter staff your card is about to expire. If you cannot obtain a document in time, ask whether they will accept the application with that item to follow. If your status lapses before an application is accepted, contact an immigration lawyer right away.

Can I travel abroad while my renewal is being processed?

Leaving Japan during processing can be risky, especially if your original period of stay ends while you are away. Re-entry rules have specific conditions, so confirm with the immigration bureau before booking any trip.

My landlord says my residence card is expired. Will I lose my apartment?

Not if your renewal application was accepted before expiry. Show your management company both sides of the card, including the "application in progress" stamp, and explain that you are in the special period. Send a copy of the new card as soon as you receive it. If you are worried about the guarantor company, our no-guarantor page explains how these companies check status.

Do I need a lawyer to fix a document problem?

For a simple missing certificate or wrong tax year, most people fix it themselves. Consider professional help if you have unpaid taxes or pension you cannot resolve quickly, a job that may not match your status, a past refusal, or if your status has already expired.
