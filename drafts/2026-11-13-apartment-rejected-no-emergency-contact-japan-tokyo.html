Apartment Rejected Because You Have No Emergency Contact in Japan? What to Do in Tokyo

You found an apartment in Tokyo, filled in the application, passed the viewing, and then the agent calls with bad news: the guarantor company or landlord will not proceed because you have "no emergency contact" (緊急連絡先, kinkyu renrakusaki). Sometimes it is phrased more vaguely: "the screening did not go through" or "they need a Japanese contact." Either way, the apartment you wanted is now on hold or gone.

This is one of the most frustrating rejections for newcomers because it has nothing to do with your income, your job, or how responsible you are. It is a box on a form that many foreigners simply cannot fill in during their first months in Japan. The good news is that it is also one of the most fixable problems in the rental process, once you understand what the landlord is actually asking for.

What actually happens

Almost every rental application in Tokyo asks for two different people, and they are easy to confuse:

  • Guarantor (連帯保証人, rentai hoshonin): a person who is legally responsible for paying your rent if you do not. Today this is usually replaced by a rent guarantee company (保証会社, hosho gaisha).
  • Emergency contact (緊急連絡先): a person who can be reached if something happens to you or the apartment and you cannot be contacted. They have no obligation to pay anything.

Many foreigners solve the guarantor problem by using a guarantee company, only to discover that the guarantee company itself requires an emergency contact. The application form typically asks for the contact's name, relationship to you, address, and phone number, and sometimes their workplace. Many forms or screening policies quietly expect that person to live in Japan and speak Japanese.

When you leave the field blank, write a parent's overseas number, or list a friend you met two weeks ago, one of three things tends to happen:

  1. The guarantee company rejects the application outright.
  2. The screening is put on hold and the agent asks you to "find someone else."
  3. The landlord accepts the guarantee company result but adds their own condition that a contact in Japan be listed.

The guarantee company may also phone your emergency contact during screening to confirm they exist and agree to be listed. If that call fails because the number is overseas, nobody answers, or the person does not understand Japanese, the application can stall even if everything else is fine.

Why it happens

It feels personal, but the emergency contact requirement comes from structural risks that landlords and guarantee companies are trying to manage.

The guarantee company needs a way to reach someone

If a tenant stops paying, disappears, or cannot be reached, the guarantee company pays the landlord and then tries to recover the money. An emergency contact gives them a second route to find the tenant. Without one, their risk goes up, so their screening becomes stricter.

Landlords fear the "unreachable tenant" scenario

Japanese landlords worry about situations such as a water leak flooding the unit below while the tenant is abroad, a tenant falling seriously ill alone in the apartment, or a tenant leaving Japan with belongings still inside. In each case the landlord needs someone to call. A contact overseas in a different time zone who speaks no Japanese does not feel like a solution to them.

Residence period versus contract period

Standard leases in Tokyo run for two years. If your residence card shows a period of stay of one year, the landlord may already be uneasy about whether you will still be in Japan for the full contract. A missing emergency contact adds to the feeling that you could leave suddenly.

Language and verification

Screening staff usually verify contacts by phone in Japanese. An English-only contact is not automatically refused, but many companies are simply not set up to handle it, so the default answer becomes "no."

Common mistakes

  • Listing someone without asking them first. If the guarantee company calls and your friend says "I don't know anything about this," the application is likely to fail and the agent may lose confidence in you.
  • Writing only an overseas number without asking the agent. Some guarantee companies accept a contact abroad, many do not. Ask before you submit, not after a rejection.
  • Confusing emergency contact with guarantor. Some people refuse to be an emergency contact because they think they will owe money. Explain clearly that an emergency contact has no payment responsibility, and show them the form.
  • Applying through a guarantee company that is not used to foreign tenants. The same applicant can be rejected by one company and approved by another. The agent usually decides which company to use, so ask about alternatives.
  • Giving up on the apartment too quickly. An "emergency contact" rejection is often a hold, not a final decision. If you supply a suitable contact within a day or two, screening can resume.

Step-by-step fix

  1. Ask the agent exactly what is missing. Say: "Is the problem that I have no emergency contact at all, or that my contact is overseas? Which conditions does the guarantee company require?" In Japanese: 「緊急連絡先はどのような条件が必要ですか？海外在住の家族でも大丈夫ですか？」 The answer tells you which of the next steps to take.
  2. Check whether your employer or school can act as contact. Many Tokyo companies that hire foreign staff, and many language schools and universities, have a person or HR team that agrees to be listed. Ask HR: "Could the company be listed as my emergency contact for my apartment application? There is no financial responsibility." A company listing often reassures the landlord more than a private individual.
  3. Ask a Japan-based acquaintance and brief them properly. A colleague, a friend from a language exchange, or a long-term foreign resident who speaks Japanese can work. Tell them the name of the guarantee company, that they may receive a call, and that they only need to confirm they know you and agree to be listed.
  4. Ask whether a family member overseas is acceptable. Some guarantee companies that specialise in foreign tenants accept a contact abroad, sometimes with an international phone number and an email address. If your agent says yes, prepare your family member to answer a call from Japan.
  5. Ask the agent to switch guarantee companies. Say: "Is there a guarantee company that is more flexible about emergency contacts for foreigners?" Agents in Tokyo who work with foreign tenants usually know which companies are easier. Expect a guarantee fee in the range of roughly 50% to 100% of one month's rent upfront, plus an annual renewal fee, depending on the company.
  6. Consider an emergency contact service. Some paid services act as your emergency contact for rental contracts. Ask the agent first whether the landlord and guarantee company accept them, because not all do.
  7. Use housing that does not require an emergency contact in Japan. Share houses, serviced apartments, and some furnished rentals aimed at foreigners have simpler screening. They are a practical bridge while you build connections and get a regular lease later. For example, [PR] CrossOneRoom offers furnished apartments in Tokyo for foreign residents.
  8. Resubmit quickly and confirm. Once you have a contact, send the details the same day and ask the agent to tell you when the guarantee company calls them. Popular Tokyo apartments rarely stay on hold for long.
Never list someone as your emergency contact without their clear agreement. A failed verification call can end the application, and it can damage your relationship with the agent for future searches.

Required documents

Prepare these before you resubmit so the agent can move immediately:

  • Residence card (在留カード, zairyu card), front and back
  • Passport, photo page and visa page
  • Proof of employment or enrolment (在職証明書 / 在学証明書) or your job offer letter
  • Proof of income, such as recent payslips or a tax withholding slip (源泉徴収票)
  • Japanese phone number and a working email address
  • Emergency contact details: full name, relationship, address, phone number, and workplace if asked
  • Written note or message from your emergency contact confirming they agree to be listed (helpful, not always required)
  • Bank account details or credit card for rent payment, if the guarantee company asks for it at screening

City / region differences

Tokyo has the largest number of agents and guarantee companies that are used to foreign tenants, especially in areas with many international residents. That means more options when one company refuses you, but also more competition for each apartment, so speed matters.

Yokohama and other Kanagawa areas often work with the same guarantee companies as Tokyo, so the same fixes apply. Commuting to Tokyo from here can open up apartments with slightly less competition.

Osaka and Kansai have their own local customs around move-in costs, but the emergency contact requirement is just as common. See our Osaka renting page for the regional details.

Smaller cities such as Nagoya, Fukuoka, Kobe, and Kyoto can be either easier or harder. Some local landlords care more about a personal introduction, which makes an employer or school contact particularly valuable. Others use national guarantee companies with the same rules as Tokyo.

Whatever the city, a stable visa status helps every part of screening. If your renewal is coming up, check our visa guide and the earlier article on why apartment applications get rejected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my emergency contact have to pay my rent if I don't?

No. An emergency contact is only a person who can be reached in an emergency. Payment responsibility belongs to the guarantor or the guarantee company. Check the form carefully, though: make sure your contact is not accidentally signing in the guarantor section.

Can I use my parents abroad as my emergency contact?

Sometimes. Guarantee companies that regularly work with foreign tenants may accept a family member overseas, especially if they can be reached by phone and email. Many standard companies prefer someone in Japan. Ask your agent before submitting.

Can my company be my emergency contact?

Often yes, and it is one of the strongest options because it also shows stable employment. Ask HR whether they allow it and who should be named. Some companies already do this routinely for foreign employees.

Will the guarantee company actually call my emergency contact?

It is common for them to call during screening to confirm the person exists and agrees to be listed. The call is usually short and in Japanese. Warn your contact in advance so they answer and respond confidently.

What if I just arrived and know nobody in Japan?

Start with your employer or school, ask your agent about guarantee companies that accept overseas contacts, and consider a share house or furnished apartment for your first months. Once you have colleagues and a few months of history in Tokyo, a regular lease becomes much easier.
