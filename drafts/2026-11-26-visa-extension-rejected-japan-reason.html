Visa Extension Rejected in Japan? The Real Reasons and What to Do Next

Opening a postcard from immigration and being told to come in, only to hear that your application for an extension of stay was not approved, is one of the most stressful moments you can have as a foreign resident in Japan. Your job, your apartment, your bank account and your daily life all depend on that residence card. The first thing to know is that a refusal is not always the end. Many denials come down to a specific, fixable problem: missing tax payments, a mismatch between your job and your visa category, or documents that did not tell a consistent story. This article walks you through what actually happens, why it happens, and the concrete steps to take while you still have time.

What Actually Happens When an Extension Is Refused

When you apply for an Extension of Period of Stay (在留期間更新許可申請) at your regional immigration services bureau (出入国在留管理局), you usually receive a receipt and a stamp on the back of your residence card showing that your application is under review. If your current period of stay expires while the application is pending, you can generally keep living in Japan under the same status until a decision is made, up to two months after the original expiry date. This is often called the special period (特例期間).

When the result is ready, you receive a notification postcard. If the answer is positive, you pay the fee and get a new card. If the answer is negative, the experience usually looks like this:

  • You are called to the counter and told the application was not permitted (不許可).
  • In many cases, staff offer you a change of status to Designated Activities (特定活動) for departure preparation (出国準備). This usually gives you a short period, often around one month, to wrap up your affairs and leave Japan.
  • The officer may give a short explanation, but often the written notice itself says very little.
  • You may be told you can reapply, or ask for the reason in more detail, but you need to ask. Staff rarely volunteer a full explanation.
Important: Do not sign or accept anything you do not understand. Ask clearly, "Could you explain the reason for the refusal?" (不許可の理由を教えていただけますか？). Write down exactly what they say. That reason is the single most valuable piece of information for your next move.

The departure-preparation status does not automatically mean you are being deported. It means immigration is giving you a lawful window to leave. However, it also means your previous status has ended, and your options inside Japan are now limited. What you do in the first few days matters a lot.

Why It Happens

Immigration decides extensions based on whether your activities match your status of residence and whether there is good reason to let you continue. Officers look at the whole picture. The most common structural reasons are below.

Unpaid taxes, health insurance or pension

Immigration increasingly checks whether you have met your public obligations. Residence tax (住民税) certificates are often part of the application, and unpaid amounts show up clearly. Gaps in National Health Insurance (国民健康保険) or National Pension (国民年金) payments, especially for freelancers and people between jobs, can lead to a refusal or a shorter period of stay.

Your job does not match your visa category

Each work status covers a specific range of activities. If you hold Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services (技術・人文知識・国際業務) but your actual work is mostly simple labor, such as working a register, factory line or warehouse, immigration may decide the activity does not fit. This often happens after a job change where the new role looks different on paper.

Income too low or employer instability

If your annual income dropped sharply, or your employer is very small, newly founded or has unclear finances, immigration may question whether you can support yourself and whether the job is genuine.

Student-specific problems

For Student (留学) status, the usual triggers are poor attendance, low grades, failing to progress, or working more part-time hours than your permission to engage in activity other than that permitted (資格外活動許可) allows. Immigration can compare your declared income with your work limit.

Spouse and family visa issues

For Spouse or Child of Japanese National (日本人の配偶者等) and similar statuses, living separately, an ongoing divorce, or inconsistencies about the relationship can lead to closer review and possible refusal.

Missing notifications and inconsistent paperwork

If you changed employers or schools and did not submit the required notification to immigration, or your address, income and employment details differ between documents, it raises doubts. Immigration does not always ask for clarification. Sometimes it simply refuses.

Common Mistakes

  1. Panicking and leaving without asking for the reason. Many people accept the departure-preparation status and book a flight without ever learning why they were refused. Without the reason, you cannot fix the problem or prepare a stronger application for a future visa.
  2. Assuming you can just apply again with the same documents. Reapplying with identical paperwork usually gets the same result. A second application only makes sense if you have actually solved the issue, for example by paying back taxes or providing a clearer job description.
  3. Ignoring the housing side. Your lease probably requires one month or more of notice to cancel (解約予告). If you wait until your last week, you may end up paying rent after you leave, losing more of your deposit, or leaving furniture and utilities in a mess.
  4. Working beyond what your new status allows. Departure-preparation status may restrict or remove your permission to work. Continuing to work without checking can create a serious problem on your record.
  5. Relying on online forums instead of a professional. Every case is different. A qualified immigration lawyer (弁護士) or administrative scrivener specializing in visas (行政書士) can tell you whether a reapplication or a change of status is realistic.

Step-by-Step Fix

  1. Ask for the reason, in detail, immediately. At the counter or by returning to the bureau, say: "I would like to understand the reason for the refusal so I can prepare properly." (今後の準備のために、不許可の理由を詳しく教えてください). Take notes, including the officer's wording. If your Japanese is limited, bring a friend or a professional who can interpret.
  2. Confirm your exact deadline and status. Check the new date printed on your residence card or passport and confirm whether you are allowed to work during this period. Ask, "Am I allowed to work during this period?" (この期間中、働くことはできますか？).
  3. Contact a visa specialist within days, not weeks. Bring your refusal notice, the original application copy, tax certificates, employment contract and residence card. Ask specifically whether the problem can be fixed and whether a reapplication within your remaining period is possible.
  4. Fix the underlying issue if you can. Typical fixes include:
    • Paying overdue residence tax, health insurance or pension, and getting payment certificates (納税証明書 and similar).
    • Getting your employer to write a detailed job description explaining how your duties match your status.
    • Submitting any missing notifications about job or school changes.
    • For students, getting a letter from your school about attendance plans and academic progress.
  5. Decide: reapply, change status, or leave and return later. With professional advice, choose one path. Sometimes the best option is leaving Japan and applying for a new Certificate of Eligibility (在留資格認定証明書) from abroad once the issue is resolved. A refusal for an extension does not necessarily stop you from coming back in the future, but a clean exit helps.
  6. Talk to your landlord or management company early. Tell them you may need to move out and ask about the notice period, move-out inspection date and deposit return method. Say: "I may need to move out at the end of next month. Could you tell me the cancellation procedure?" (来月末に退去する可能性があります。解約の手続きを教えてください). Read our guide on hidden costs so you know what may be deducted from your deposit.
  7. If you need to stay in Japan short-term while reapplying, consider flexible housing. If you have already given notice on your apartment but are still waiting for a second decision, a furnished share house with short contracts avoids being locked into a two-year lease. [PR] Oakhouse share houses offer furnished rooms on flexible terms in many cities.
  8. Close everything properly if you leave. Cancel utilities, internet and phone; file a moving-out notification (転出届) at your city office; handle your residence tax; and return your residence card at the airport as instructed. Leaving cleanly makes future visa applications far easier.

Required Documents

Whether you reapply, change status, or consult a professional, gather these documents. Exact requirements differ by status, so confirm with immigration or your advisor.

  • Residence card (在留カード)
  • Passport (パスポート)
  • Refusal notice or any document received at the counter (不許可通知 or related papers)
  • Copy of your original extension application (在留期間更新許可申請書の控え)
  • Residence tax certificate and tax payment certificate (住民税の課税証明書・納税証明書)
  • Proof of health insurance and pension payments (国民健康保険・国民年金の納付記録)
  • Employment contract and detailed job description from your employer (雇用契約書・職務内容説明書)
  • Employer's company documents, if requested (登記事項証明書, financial statements)
  • For students: enrollment and attendance certificates (在学証明書・出席証明書) and transcripts (成績証明書)
  • For spouse visas: marriage certificate and proof of shared life (婚姻証明書, joint residence certificate 住民票)
  • Your apartment lease agreement (賃貸借契約書), for planning move-out or staying

City / Region Differences

Immigration law is national, but the practical experience depends on which bureau handles your case.

  • Tokyo: The Tokyo Regional Immigration Services Bureau in Shinagawa handles a huge volume. Expect long waits and very short conversations at the counter. Arrive early on the day you are called and bring someone who can help in Japanese. Tokyo also has many visa-focused law offices with English support.
  • Osaka: The Osaka bureau covers much of the Kansai region. Waiting times can also be long. If you live in Osaka and need to move out quickly, read our Osaka renting page for local rental patterns and notice periods.
  • Nagoya, Fukuoka and other cities: Regional bureaus and branch offices tend to be less crowded, so you may get a slightly longer explanation. However, fewer English-speaking professionals are nearby, so you may need to consult online.
  • Smaller cities: Branch offices sometimes forward complex cases to the regional bureau, which can make timelines harder to predict. Start any reapplication process as early as possible.

For housing, renting while your visa is in doubt is hard everywhere. Landlords and guarantor companies check your period of stay. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I appeal a visa extension refusal in Japan?

There is generally no simple appeal process for an extension refusal like there is for some other government decisions. In practice, the realistic options are fixing the problem and reapplying if time allows, changing to a different suitable status, or leaving and applying again from abroad. A court challenge is possible in theory but is rare, slow and requires a lawyer. Consult a visa specialist before deciding.

How long do I have to leave Japan after a refusal?

It depends on what immigration gives you. Many people receive a departure-preparation status, often for around one month, but you must check the exact date on your card or passport. Do not rely on what others received. Overstaying even by a few days can cause serious problems for future visas.

Will a refusal stop me from getting a visa in the future?

Not necessarily. A refusal is recorded, but if you leave on time and fix the underlying issue, many people successfully return later with a new Certificate of Eligibility. Leaving cleanly, with taxes paid and paperwork in order, gives you the best chance.

What happens to my apartment and deposit?

Your lease does not end automatically because of a visa refusal. You still need to give notice according to your contract, usually one to two months, and complete a move-out inspection. Tell your management company as early as possible. If you cannot stay until the end of the notice period, you may still owe rent for it. Our rejection help page also explains how visa status affects rental screening.

Can I reapply for an extension while on departure-preparation status?

In some cases people do reapply or apply for a change of status, but success depends on whether the original problem has really been solved. Submitting the same documents again rarely works. A visa specialist can tell you whether a new application is realistic in your remaining time.
