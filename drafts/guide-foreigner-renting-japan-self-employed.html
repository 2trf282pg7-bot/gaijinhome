Self-Employed Foreigner Rejected for Apartment in Japan? Exact Fixes and Workarounds

You run your own business, freelance for clients at home and abroad, or hold a Business Manager visa. You earn a decent income, maybe more than many salaried applicants. Yet the apartment screening came back negative, sometimes more than once. Being self-employed is one of the toughest profiles in Japanese rental screening, and being a foreign national on top of that compounds it. The system is built around company employees with stable monthly salaries. Once you understand what the screeners want to see, you can rebuild your application to match.

What Actually Happens to Self-Employed Applicants

The rejection usually looks like one of these:

  • The guarantor company (保証会社) declines without explanation, even though your income is strong.
  • The agent asks for documents you do not have yet, such as a tax return filed in Japan, and the application stops there.
  • The landlord asks for a larger deposit, several months' prepaid rent, or a Japanese co-guarantor.
  • You are told that "sole proprietors (個人事業主) are difficult" for this property, before you even apply.

If you only recently started your business or moved to Japan, the most likely problem is not your income. It is that your income is not yet visible in the Japanese documents screeners are trained to read.

Why Self-Employed Foreigners Get Rejected

Screening is designed for salaried employees

Guarantor companies typically judge stability through employer name, years of service, and annual salary. A company employee's payslips are simple to verify. A freelancer's income can vary month to month and comes from many sources, so screeners default to caution.

No Japanese tax history yet

For self-employed people, the key proof of income in Japan is the filed tax return (確定申告書) and the municipal tax certificates that follow from it. If you arrived in Japan recently, or your first tax year has not closed, you may have no Japanese return at all. Foreign tax returns and invoices from overseas clients are hard for screeners to evaluate, even when they are perfectly valid.

Low declared income from deductions

Freelancers often reduce taxable income through business expenses. That is legitimate, but screening usually looks at the income figure on the tax documents, and an aggressive expense strategy can make you look like a low earner on paper.

Visa period and business status uncertainty

Business Manager (経営・管理) holders often start with a short period of stay, frequently one year, and renewal depends on the business continuing. Freelancers on other statuses may face questions about whether their activities match their visa. Landlords considering a two-year lease see risk in both cases.

Registered office versus residence

If you plan to run a business from home, register a company at the address, or receive clients there, many leases prohibit it. Mentioning business use at the wrong moment can end an application immediately.

Key tip: Separate your home search from your business. Apply as an individual living in the apartment, show personal income and savings clearly, and handle any business registration address separately unless the property explicitly allows business use (事務所利用可 or SOHO可).

Common Mistakes Self-Employed Applicants Make

  1. Submitting invoices instead of tax documents. A stack of invoices or a spreadsheet of client payments is hard for a guarantor company to evaluate. Official documents matter more than raw evidence.
  2. Writing "freelance" with no detail on the application. An occupation field that just says "freelance" or "self-employed" looks vague. Give your trade name, business type, years in business, and main client types.
  3. Not mentioning savings. Many self-employed applicants have substantial savings but never show them. A bank balance certificate can offset irregular income.
  4. Applying at the top of your budget. If screeners already see you as higher risk, a high rent-to-income ratio makes rejection likely. Applying a bracket lower often changes the result.
  5. Saying the apartment will also be your office. Unless the listing allows business use, this can trigger an immediate refusal.

Step-by-Step Fix: How to Get Approved

  1. Get your Japanese tax paperwork in order. If you have filed at least one return in Japan, request a tax certificate (課税証明書) and a tax payment certificate (納税証明書) from your city office or tax office. These are the most persuasive documents you have. If you have not filed yet, gather your notification of business opening (開業届) as filed with the tax office, and your company registration certificate (履歴事項全部証明書) if you run a corporation.
  2. Show savings directly. Get a bank balance certificate (残高証明書) from your bank. A balance that covers a year or more of rent can significantly change how screeners view you. Some landlords and guarantor companies accept savings as an alternative to steady income.
  3. Prepare a short business summary. One page: business name, what you do, when you started, main clients or contracts (without confidential details), and average monthly income. Attach copies of ongoing client contracts if you have them. The agent can submit this with your application.
  4. Ask about prepaid rent or a higher deposit. Ask the agent: "Yachin no zennō ya shikikin no zōgaku de sōdan dekimasu ka?" (家賃の前納や敷金の増額で相談できますか？ "Can we discuss prepaying rent or increasing the deposit?"). Some landlords accept this from self-employed applicants. Get any prepayment terms in writing and use a reputable agency.
  5. Use your own company for a corporate lease. If you run a corporation (株式会社 or 合同会社), the company itself can sometimes sign as tenant under a corporate lease (法人契約). This depends on the company's financial history, so it works better once your company has completed a fiscal year, but it is worth asking about.
  6. Target landlords who are flexible. UR housing allows applicants who do not meet the standard income test to qualify through savings above a set level; check the current rules in our UR guide. Furnished apartments and monthly rentals often check income less strictly. [PR] CrossOneRoom furnished Tokyo apartments cater to international residents and can be a practical bridge while you build Japanese tax history.
  7. Build credit and history in parallel. Keep a Japanese bank account active, pay utilities and phone bills on time, and file your tax return properly at the next deadline. Our guide on passing the credit check in Japan explains which records matter.
  8. Reapply after your first Japanese tax filing. Many self-employed foreigners find that once they have a filed return and the resulting tax certificate, standard apartments become reachable. If you can, time your long-term search for after that point.

Required Documents for Self-Employed Applicants

  • Residence card (在留カード) showing status and period of stay
  • Passport
  • Copy of your filed tax return (確定申告書の控え), ideally with the tax office receipt stamp or e-filing confirmation
  • Tax certificate (課税証明書) and/or tax payment certificate (納税証明書)
  • Notification of business opening (開業届の控え) for sole proprietors
  • Company registration certificate (履歴事項全部証明書) for corporation owners
  • Bank balance certificate (残高証明書) and recent bank statements
  • Copies of major client contracts or engagement letters
  • One-page business summary (事業概要) in English and simple Japanese
  • Emergency contact in Japan (緊急連絡先) who has agreed to be listed

City and Region Differences

Tokyo: Has the most freelancers and startups, and more agencies and landlords familiar with self-employed tenants and SOHO-friendly properties. It also has the highest rents, so the income-to-rent gap is the biggest hurdle. Startup-friendly districts often have buildings marketed for small offices or home offices, but always confirm residential and business use in writing.

Osaka: Lower rents make your income go further in screening terms, and many local landlords are used to small business owners, since the city has a large population of shopkeepers and sole proprietors. Our Osaka renting guide covers neighborhoods and costs.

Fukuoka: Popular with startups and has promoted itself to entrepreneurs, including foreign founders. Rents are relatively moderate, which helps your screening ratio, and some local programs support new businesses, though they do not guarantee housing.

Smaller cities: Landlords are often individuals who may accept a stronger deposit or a personal meeting in place of strict documentation. Rents are lower, so savings proof goes further.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I rent in Japan if I have only foreign tax returns?

It is possible but harder. Many guarantor companies are not set up to evaluate overseas tax documents. Pair them with a Japanese bank balance certificate, client contracts, and a clear business summary, and consider furnished or monthly apartments until you have filed a Japanese return.

Does a Business Manager visa make renting easier?

Not automatically. The visa shows you are permitted to run a business, but landlords still look at income documents and the remaining period of stay. If your first period of stay is short, landlords may hesitate. Strong savings and a company that has started trading help offset that.

Can I register my company at my apartment address?

Only if your lease allows it. Many standard residential leases forbid business registration or office use. Look for properties described as office-use permitted or SOHO-friendly, and get the permission in writing before you register. Registering without permission can breach your lease.

Should I lower my declared expenses to show higher income?

No. Your tax filing should reflect your actual expenses correctly. Instead, offset lower declared income with savings proof, contracts, and prepayment offers. If you are unsure how to file, consult a licensed tax accountant (税理士) rather than adjusting figures for screening purposes.

What if every guarantor company has rejected me?

Look at options that do not depend on standard guarantor screening, such as UR housing, share houses, and furnished rentals. Our guide to alternatives when a guarantor company refuses you lists the main routes in detail.
