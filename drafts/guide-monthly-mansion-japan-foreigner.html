Monthly Mansion Application Rejected in Japan as a Foreigner? What Actually Works

Monthly mansions (マンスリーマンション) are supposed to be the easy option: furnished, short-term, no long lease, no complicated guarantor. So it stings when a booking is declined, the operator asks for documents you do not have, or you discover the place does not let you register your address after all.

Monthly mansions follow different rules from standard apartments, and they reject people for different reasons. If you understand those reasons, you can usually find an operator that will take you, often within days. This guide focuses only on the monthly and short-term furnished market.

What Actually Happens

Foreigners typically hit one of these walls:

  • Booking declined without explanation. You submit an online request and receive a short message saying the unit is "not available" or that they "cannot accept" your booking.
  • Request for a Japanese address or contact. The form asks for a current address in Japan, a Japanese phone number, or an emergency contact in Japan, which you do not have yet.
  • Visa or entry questions. The operator asks for your residence card, but you are arriving on a short-term visitor status and do not have one.
  • Payment problems. The operator requires full prepayment by Japanese bank transfer, or will not accept your foreign card.
  • Approved, but you cannot register your address. You move in, then find the property does not allow resident registration (住民登録), which blocks your bank account and phone contract.

Why Monthly Mansion Operators Reject Foreigners

The contract is legally different

Monthly mansions are commonly offered under a fixed-term lease (定期借家契約) or as a usage agreement, rather than an ordinary two-year lease. Some short-stay properties for under a month may operate under hotel or vacation rental (民泊) rules, which come with their own identity checks. Operators choose who to accept based on their own policies, and some prefer guests they can easily screen.

Prepayment replaces the guarantor, but not always

Most monthly mansions ask for the full stay or at least the first month paid upfront, and that prepayment is what often replaces a guarantor. However, longer stays may still go through screening, and some operators use a guarantor company or ask for an emergency contact.

Identity and residence status

Operators commonly want to confirm who will live in the unit. Residence card holders are usually straightforward. Short-term visitors without a residence card are accepted by many operators, but some decline them, especially for longer stays, because they cannot verify a future residence status.

Fear of overcrowding and subletting

Monthly units are small, and operators worry about extra people staying, or the unit being re-rented as a short-term rental. Unclear answers about how many people will stay, or why you need the unit, can trigger a decline.

Language and emergencies

Many smaller operators have no English support and worry about handling problems such as water leaks, lost keys or noise complaints with a guest who cannot speak Japanese.

Common Mistakes

  1. Booking only the cheapest listing. Budget operators often have the strictest acceptance rules and the least English support. A slightly more expensive operator used to foreign guests may approve you immediately.
  2. Not asking about resident registration before booking. If you plan to open a bank account or sign a phone contract, a monthly mansion that bans address registration will trap you.
  3. Leaving the purpose field vague. "Stay" or a blank field looks suspicious. "Relocation for work starting in April, searching for a long-term apartment" sounds like exactly the customer they want.
  4. Forgetting the extra charges. Cleaning fees, utility flat fees, linen fees and contract fees can significantly raise the total. Compare total cost, not the nightly or monthly headline.
  5. Treating a monthly mansion as a long-term home. Monthly rates generally cost more per month than standard leases. Use it as a bridge while you search.

Step-by-Step: What Actually Works

  1. Decide what you need the stay to do. If you only need a roof for a few weeks, any furnished operator works. If you need to register your address, open a bank account and get a phone, you need a unit that explicitly allows resident registration. Write this down before you search.
  2. Contact operators directly with three questions. Ask: "Do you accept foreign guests?" (外国籍の方も利用可能ですか？), "Can I register my address here?" (住民登録は可能ですか？), and "Which payment methods do you accept?" (支払い方法は何がありますか？). Their answers filter out unsuitable operators in one round.
  3. Give a clear purpose and timeline in your request. Include your job or school, arrival date, length of stay, number of occupants, and that you are searching for a long-term rental. Attach your employment or enrollment letter if you have one.
  4. Offer full prepayment. If you can, offer to pay the whole stay in advance. For many operators, this removes most of the risk they are worried about.
  5. Use your employer or school if possible. Many operators have corporate plans where the company books for you. Ask HR whether they have a preferred monthly mansion provider; corporate bookings are often approved quickly.
  6. Consider alternatives with similar flexibility. Share houses often allow short minimum stays, accept foreigners by default, and frequently allow address registration. [PR] Oakhouse share houses do not require a guarantor. See also our share house guide for Tokyo.
  7. Use the stay to prepare your long-term application. Register your address, get a Japanese phone number, open a bank account, and gather salary slips. When you apply for a standard apartment, you will be a far stronger applicant. Our complete renting guide walks through that next step.

Key tip: Before paying anything, get the operator's answer about resident registration in writing. A monthly mansion that lets you register your address can save you weeks of bank and phone headaches; one that does not can leave you stuck.

Required Documents

  • Passport (required by almost every operator)
  • Residence card (在留カード), if you already have one
  • Flight booking or entry stamp, if arriving as a short-term visitor
  • Certificate of employment (在職証明書), offer letter (内定通知書) or enrollment certificate (在学証明書)
  • Credit card for prepayment, or proof you can pay by bank transfer
  • Japanese phone number, or an international number plus email you check daily
  • Emergency contact (緊急連絡先), ideally in Japan; some operators accept an overseas contact
  • Number and names of all occupants
  • Corporate booking form, if your employer is reserving the unit

City and Region Differences

Tokyo

Tokyo has by far the largest monthly mansion market, with many operators clustered around business hubs such as Shinjuku, Shinagawa, Ikebukuro and Ueno. Competition and prices peak in the spring relocation season (roughly February to April) when companies book units for transferring staff, so book early if you arrive then. Foreigner-friendly operators are easiest to find here.

Osaka

Osaka has a healthy monthly mansion market around Umeda, Namba, Shin-Osaka and Tennoji, and rates are often somewhat lower than in central Tokyo. Some short-stay properties in Osaka operate as vacation rentals, so check carefully whether address registration is possible. See our Osaka guide for local tips.

Other cities

Fukuoka, Nagoya, Sapporo and other regional cities have monthly mansions mainly near main stations and business districts. Choice is smaller, and English support is less common, so contact operators early and consider employer-arranged housing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I book a monthly mansion before I arrive in Japan?

Yes, many operators accept bookings from overseas, especially with prepayment by credit card. Some only accept guests with a residence card or a Japanese contact, so confirm acceptance of overseas bookings before you pay.

Do monthly mansions require a guarantor?

Usually not for shorter stays, because prepayment covers the risk. For longer stays, some operators run screening or use a guarantor company. If you are being asked for a guarantor, ask whether full prepayment can replace it.

Can I register my address at a monthly mansion?

It depends on the property. Some allow resident registration, others prohibit it, and properties run as vacation rentals often cannot be used. Always ask before booking if you need to register your address for a bank account or phone contract.

Is a monthly mansion cheaper than a hotel?

For stays of a month or longer, monthly mansions are often cheaper than hotels and give you a kitchen and washing machine. They usually cost more per month than a standard lease once fees are added, but you avoid the deposit, key money and agent fees of a normal apartment.

What if every operator keeps declining me?

Try operators that specifically market to foreign residents, ask your employer or school for a corporate booking, or switch to a share house, which accepts foreigners by default. If you are being rejected for standard apartments too, see our guide on what to do after a rejection.
