Apartment Denied in Japan Because You Have No Bank Account? What to Do

You need an address to open a bank account. You need a bank account to rent an apartment. If you have just arrived in Japan, you may already be stuck in this loop: the agent tells you the guarantor company needs a Japanese account for rent payments, and the bank tells you to come back when you have a registered address and a phone number.

This is one of the most frustrating parts of moving to Japan, but it is a known problem with known workarounds. The goal is to break the loop in the right order, using temporary housing or alternative payment methods so you are not blocked for weeks.

What Actually Happens

The denial usually shows up at one of three points:

  • At application. The form asks for your bank name, branch and account number for rent withdrawal (口座振替, kōza furikae). Without it, the agent says they cannot submit.
  • At guarantor screening. The guarantor company collects rent from you by direct debit and passes it to the landlord. No account means no way to collect, so they decline or put the application on hold.
  • At contract signing. You were tentatively approved, but signing requires the direct debit form (口座振替依頼書) stamped or signed with your bank details, and you cannot complete it.

Often the agent frames this as a rejection, when in practice it is a missing piece of paperwork. That distinction matters, because a missing piece can be supplied.

Why It Happens

Rent in Japan is built around automatic bank debits

Most Japanese rentals collect rent by monthly direct debit or bank transfer. Guarantor companies in particular tend to require direct debit, since automatic collection is central to how they manage the risk of covering your rent. Paying in cash every month is rare for standard apartments.

Banks have their own requirements for foreigners

To open an account, Japanese banks generally want a residence card showing your registered address, a Japanese phone number, and sometimes proof of employment or study. Some banks are stricter with people who have been in Japan for less than six months, because under foreign exchange rules newly arrived people can be treated as non-residents for banking purposes. Policies differ by bank and even by branch.

The address problem

Your residence card address is registered at the city or ward office after you have somewhere to live. Hotels are generally not accepted as a residential address for registration. So if you are in a hotel, you may not be able to register, which blocks the bank account, which blocks the apartment.

Overseas accounts and cards are not a direct substitute

Most guarantor companies cannot debit a foreign bank account. Foreign credit cards may or may not be accepted for rent, depending on the guarantor company and the payment system they use.

Common Mistakes

  1. Waiting in a hotel until a bank account appears. Without a registerable address, the account may never come. You need to break the loop with an address first.
  2. Applying at only one bank. Policies vary a lot. If one branch refuses, another bank, or even another branch, may accept you.
  3. Not asking about card payment. Some guarantor companies and management companies accept rent by credit card. Agents may not mention it unless asked.
  4. Letting a good apartment go without asking for a deadline extension. Many agents can hold an application while you finish opening an account, if you show you are actively doing it.
  5. Using a friend's account to pay rent. This creates confusion and risk for both of you, and the contract may not allow it. Avoid it.

Step-by-Step Fix

  1. Ask the agent what exactly is missing. Say: "Is a bank account required at application, or only at contract signing? Can I pay by credit card instead?" (申込時に口座は必要ですか？契約時までで大丈夫ですか？クレジットカード払いは可能ですか？). Often the account is needed only before the first debit, giving you a few weeks.
  2. Ask if the application can go ahead with your bank details to follow. Many agents accept the direct debit form after the contract, especially when the first months' rent is paid upfront with the initial costs. Get this confirmed in writing by email or message.
  3. Secure a registerable address quickly. If you are in a hotel, move into housing where you can register your address at the ward office: a share house, a monthly mansion that permits resident registration, or employer housing. Ask the operator directly: "Can I register my address here (住民登録できますか)?" Our share house guide and monthly mansion guide explain these options.
  4. Register your address and get a Japanese phone number. Go to the ward or city office within 14 days of moving in. Get a Japanese mobile number at the same time if you do not already have one, because banks commonly ask for one.
  5. Open an account at a bank used to foreign customers. Japan Post Bank (ゆうちょ銀行) is widely used by new arrivals and has branches everywhere, though its rules for newcomers can still vary. Some city banks and online banks also accept foreign residents. Bring your residence card, phone, and employment or student documents. If your employer has a payroll bank, ask HR for an introduction letter, which can help.
  6. Return to the agent with your bank details. Submit the direct debit form. If the original apartment is gone, you are now a much stronger applicant for the next one.
  7. If you need housing that does not depend on a Japanese account at all, consider furnished rentals where the operator handles payment by card or transfer. [PR] CrossOneRoom furnished apartments in Tokyo are aimed at foreign residents. Confirm payment methods with any operator before booking.
Key tip: Fix the address first, not the bank account. A short stay in housing where you can register your address unlocks the residence card address, then the phone, then the bank account, then the standard apartment. Trying to do it in reverse keeps you stuck.

Required Documents

For opening a bank account and finalizing your rental, have these ready:

  • Residence card (在留カード) with your registered address on the back
  • Passport
  • Certificate of residence (住民票) if the bank or agent requests it
  • Japanese mobile phone number
  • Certificate of employment (在職証明書) or employment contract, or enrollment certificate (在学証明書) for students
  • Personal seal (印鑑, inkan), as some banks and contracts still ask for one, though many now accept a signature
  • Bank's direct debit form (口座振替依頼書) from the agent or guarantor company
  • Bank cash card or passbook (通帳) showing the branch and account number
  • Credit card, if the guarantor company accepts card payment for rent
  • Proof of savings, such as an overseas bank statement, to show you can cover initial costs

City and Region Differences

Tokyo

Tokyo has the most banks and branches used to serving foreign customers, and the most share houses and furnished apartments where you can register your address quickly. This makes it the easiest place to break the address-to-bank loop, often within one or two weeks. Agents in central areas also tend to be more familiar with card payment options.

Osaka

Osaka has a solid network of Japan Post Bank and regional bank branches, and share houses in areas like Namba and Tennoji. Initial costs and deposit customs may differ from Tokyo; see our Osaka guide.

Smaller cities

Outside the major cities, fewer agents have experience with this issue, but employers often step in, providing housing or company introductions to a local bank. Regional banks may be more conservative, so Japan Post Bank is frequently the practical first choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I rent an apartment in Japan with only a foreign bank account?

For standard apartments, it is difficult, because guarantor companies usually collect rent by Japanese direct debit. Some operators of furnished rentals and share houses accept international card payments or transfers, which makes them the most realistic option until you open a Japanese account.

Can I pay rent by credit card instead?

Sometimes. Certain guarantor companies and management companies accept rent by credit card, occasionally with a fee. It depends on the property, so ask the agent directly before applying, and check whether a foreign-issued card is accepted.

How long does it take to open a bank account as a newcomer?

Once you have a registered address and a Japanese phone number, many people open an account on the same day at the branch, with the cash card arriving by post within one to a few weeks. Without an address, it may not be possible at all, which is why getting an address comes first.

Is a bank account rejection the same as failing the guarantor screening?

Not necessarily. A missing account is often a paperwork issue rather than a judgment about you. Ask the agent whether the screening itself was passed. If it was, you may only need to supply your bank details before the first rent payment. If screening failed for other reasons, see our guide on what to do after a rejection.

Can my employer help?

Yes, often. HR departments can provide an introduction letter for a bank, confirm your employment for the guarantor company, or in some cases sign a corporate lease so that rent is handled by the company. Ask early, since these processes can take time.
