Why Foreigners Get Rejected for Apartments in Osaka — And What To Do Next

Osaka is friendly, lively, and generally more affordable than Tokyo, which is why so many foreigners choose it for work, study, or a fresh start. But the apartment search can be surprisingly tough. You may have been told "外国人不可" (foreigners not accepted) for half the listings you asked about, or your application was declined after you had already planned the move. Osaka has its own rental customs, its own mix of landlords, and a few contract features that confuse newcomers. Once you understand them, the city becomes much easier to navigate.

What Actually Happens in an Osaka Rejection

There are two typical patterns. In the first, you never get to apply: the agent checks with the management company and comes back saying the owner does not accept foreign tenants. In the second, you apply, pay nothing yet, and wait several days before hearing that the screening did not pass. Sometimes a third pattern appears: the application passes, but the move-in cost estimate (初期費用の見積もり) includes deductions or fees you did not expect, and the deal falls apart when you hesitate.

Osaka also has a large number of older buildings owned by individuals, mixed with newer managed properties in areas like Umeda and Namba. The older buildings are often cheaper but more likely to have owners who prefer tenants they can communicate with easily in Japanese.

Why It Happens: Osaka-Specific Reasons

Owner preferences in older housing stock

Many traditional neighborhoods have small apartment buildings managed directly by elderly owners or long-standing local agencies. These owners may worry about language, garbage rules, or noise. The refusal is usually not about your finances at all.

Guarantor company screening

As in other big cities, most managed properties require a guarantor company (保証会社). Kansai uses a range of guarantor companies, and some have stricter criteria for foreign applicants, such as minimum remaining visa period or a Japanese-speaking emergency contact.

Short-term residence and the tourism economy

Osaka has seen strong growth in tourism and short-term rentals. Some landlords have become cautious about anyone they think might sublet or run an unlicensed vacation rental (民泊). If your application suggests frequent travel or multiple occupants, expect questions. Never sublet without written permission.

Competition in popular districts

Areas near Umeda, Namba, Shinsaibashi and Honmachi attract young professionals. When an owner has several applicants, foreign applicants with short visa periods or new jobs can lose out.

Contract features that trip people up

Osaka and the wider Kansai region have historically used a system where part of the deposit is deducted on move-out by agreement (敷引き, shikibiki), and some contracts use a large deposit called 保証金 (hoshōkin). Many modern contracts now follow the same deposit and key money (礼金) system as Tokyo, but you will still see both. Misunderstanding these terms is a common reason applicants hesitate or withdraw.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming Osaka works exactly like Tokyo. Deposit terms and renewal fees can differ. Ask about 敷引き and 更新料 directly.
  • Focusing only on Namba and Umeda. Excellent neighborhoods just a few stops away are often more open to foreign tenants.
  • Not mentioning how many people will live there. Adding a partner or roommate after approval can breach the contract.
  • Using a portal listing as proof of availability. Listings stay online after units are taken. Ask the agent to confirm availability and foreigner acceptance first.
  • Giving up after one agency. Agencies in Osaka differ a lot in how many foreigner-friendly owners they work with.

Step-by-Step Fix

  1. Find agents who regularly place foreign tenants. Ask your employer, school, or other foreign residents which agencies they used. When you call, ask: 「外国籍の入居者の対応実績はありますか？」 ("Do you have experience with foreign tenants?").
  2. Request a pre-check before every viewing. Ask the agent to confirm with the management company that foreigners are accepted and which guarantor company is used. This avoids repeated rejections.
  3. Ask for the full move-in cost estimate up front. Request a written estimate showing deposit, key money, any 敷引き or 保証金 terms, guarantor fees, insurance and agent fee. Compare it with our hidden costs guide and our article on key money.
  4. Strengthen your application with a Kansai-based emergency contact. A coworker, manager or Japanese friend in Osaka is ideal. Some guarantor companies accept a company HR contact.
  5. Show stability. Bring a certificate of employment and, if your visa expires within a year, evidence of renewal or your employer's confirmation that your contract continues.
  6. Consider UR housing. The Osaka area has many UR developments, and UR does not require a guarantor, key money or renewal fees. See our UR application guide.
  7. Use a share house as a stepping stone. If you need to move now, a share house lets you get an address and bank account in place while you search properly. [PR] Oakhouse share houses require no Japanese guarantor; check current availability in Kansai on their site.
  8. Reapply in a less competitive area. Use the neighborhood table below to choose areas with good transit and more flexible owners.
Key tip: In Osaka, always ask "Is there 敷引き in this contract?" before you apply. Knowing how much of your deposit is non-refundable lets you compare properties fairly and avoids last-minute surprises.

Required Documents

  • Residence card (在留カード), front and back
  • Passport (パスポート)
  • Certificate of employment (在職証明書) or employment contract (雇用契約書)
  • Last 2–3 months of pay slips (給与明細)
  • Tax certificate (課税証明書) or withholding tax slip (源泉徴収票)
  • Certificate of residence (住民票), if requested
  • Student ID (学生証) and certificate of enrollment (在学証明書) for students
  • Emergency contact details in Japan (緊急連絡先), ideally in Kansai
  • Bank balance certificate (残高証明書) if income is low or irregular
  • Personal seal (印鑑) or signature, depending on the contract

Osaka Neighborhoods, Rents and Local Quirks

As a rough guide, a 1K or studio in central Osaka often ranges from about 50,000 to 85,000 yen depending on age and location, with older or more outlying units below that. Family-sized units cost more. Always check current listings.

Local quirks: Garbage rules differ by city and ward, so ask for the local sorting guide. Many older Osaka buildings have no elevator above a few floors. Some properties near entertainment areas have strict rules on guests. If you work for a company in Osaka, ask HR whether it has partner agencies or a corporate lease scheme (社宅 / 借り上げ社宅), which can bypass individual screening.

Compared with Tokyo: Osaka rents are generally lower and renewal fees have traditionally been less common, though some newer contracts include them. Tokyo has more English-only agencies; Osaka relies more on agencies with mixed-language staff. For a broader overview, see our Osaka page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do so many Osaka listings say foreigners are not accepted?

Many smaller buildings are owner-managed, and some owners worry about communication, garbage rules or subletting. It is a preference, not a reflection of you. Focus your search on agencies and properties that confirm foreigner acceptance before you view.

What is 敷引き and will I get my deposit back?

敷引き is an agreed amount deducted from your deposit when you move out, regardless of the apartment's condition. It is written in the contract, so you will generally not get that portion back. Ask for the exact amount before applying and compare it with properties that use a standard refundable deposit.

Do Osaka apartments charge renewal fees?

Traditionally less often than in Tokyo, but it depends on the contract. Some properties now charge a renewal fee every two years. Check the 更新料 line in the important matters explanation before you sign.

Is UR housing a good option in Osaka?

Yes, for many foreigners. UR has many developments around Osaka, requires no guarantor or key money, and screens mainly on income or savings. Units are often in larger estates, sometimes further from the center, so check commute times.

How long does screening usually take in Osaka?

Often a few business days to about a week, depending on the guarantor company and how quickly your employer and emergency contact answer verification calls. Tell them in advance so they pick up. If it takes much longer, ask the agent whether more documents are needed.
